# Responses

A person scans a response before they read it: they take in the first words of each paragraph and line, and give up once what they need does not stand out. Put what they need where that glance lands.

- **Lead with the answer:** what the person asked for or must act on, such as the outcome, the decision or the question that waits on them. It frames the details that follow. Each sentence after it carries what they act on or need to trust the answer, most important first, so the response can stop anywhere and still have said what matters most. The steps that led there appear only as evidence.
- **Front-load every part.** Each paragraph holds one idea and opens with it, and each sentence, list item, heading and link opens with the words that carry its point, since a scanning person reads little past them. A heading names what its part holds.
- **Use plain, neutral words.** Call each thing what the person or the project calls it, choose the everyday word over the formal one, and define each term they have not met where it first appears; an expert reads plain words faster too, and so does a person reading in a second language. Name who does what, and state a condition positively, not as a double negative or an exception to an exception; write a negative in full, such as "do not", since a contracted one is easy to misread as its opposite. State facts without praise, hype or reassurance, since a person who has to discount a claim slows down and trusts the rest less.
- **Put each state in words,** such as passed or failed, not in a symbol or colour alone, which not every person can see or tell apart.
- **Give each sentence one point,** and split any sentence a person would have to read twice to follow.
- **Open each list with a complete sentence** that says what it holds, unless the heading right above it already does, and number a list only when its order matters.
- **Use a table only to compare items that each carry three or more facts.** Open it with a complete sentence that says what it compares. Items that each need reasoning form a list, since a person follows reasoning down a list but has to read across a table's row to piece it together.
- **Keep each kind of answer in the format it first took** in the conversation, until the person asks for another, since the person has learned where that format puts each fact and finds it there again without rereading.
- **Link each thing you name that has a page of its own,** once in each response, where the person needs it, with its name or title as the link text, so the link reads clearly on its own and lets the person check the claim. Give a definition or a step in the text instead of sending the person away for it.
- **Bold only what the person must see:** the answer's key phrase and each question or action that waits on them, since each extra emphasis dilutes the rest until none stands out.
